LANE — authored natively, NOT a translation

English-lane signals for renting in Malaysia: tenant-side (repairs, deposit return, rent increases, lockout threats) and landlord-side (chasing rent, lawful recovery of possession). Mechanisms and the legal boundaries live in [[scenarios/sc17-landlord-tenant/core]].

### The repair request that gets "I'll sort it out next week"

**Context** — You reported a fault (leak, water heater, wiring) and the landlord replies with an open-ended "soon". The default Malaysia channel is WhatsApp text.

**Reading** — (1) Genuine scheduling lag; (2) no booking exists; they're betting you'll get a handyman yourself and pay; (3) they've classified it as a small item not worth a call-out fee.

**Response options**
1. Add urgency to the fact: "Water has been leaking since last night; two more days and it damages the flooring. Please give me a confirmed plumber slot by tomorrow."
2. Make the delay expensive in writing: "Noted — reported X date, you said you'd arrange (screenshot saved). If no one comes by X+3, I'll arrange a plumber myself and submit the invoice under clause X of the TA."
3. State the boundary: "Discharge pipes fall under main drains (landlord's obligation in the TA). I've shut the valve and recorded a video; the plumber's invoice follows the agreed split."

**Pitfalls** — Keeping silent mental score. Every "next week" needs one written sentence in reply, or you'll be told months later "you never complained."

**Examples** — constructed example: Tenant reports a leaking sink twice, gets "later". Sends option 2; the same evening the landlord forwards a plumber's number. Written consequences produce schedules.

### The repainting deduction at move-out

**Context** — Landlord deducts RM800–2,000 from the deposit because "the paint has faded, I have to repaint the whole unit."

**Reading** — (1) The TA genuinely requires the tenant to repaint; (2) no such clause — they're passing renovation cost to you; (3) the clause only says "good repair", which full repainting far exceeds.

**Response options**
1. Invoke the wear-and-tear line: "Sun-faded paint and light scuffs are normal wear and tear — not deductible. Unless the TA expressly requires repainting, this stays out of my deposit."
2. Demand itemisation: "If you insist on deducting, please give me an itemised list: area, rooms, per-item cost — matched against my move-in photos of the same walls."
3. Settle at half: "I accept some wear; I'll pay RM300 and we close it today."

**Pitfalls** — Arguing about "how clean is clean". The only boundaries that count: explicit TA clause, normal wear and tear, and the move-in photos. No move-in photos means no case.

**Examples** — constructed example: "Repainting the walls, 800 is fair." Tenant sends option 2 with a move-in photo showing sun fade; the deduction is withdrawn the same night.

### Deposit ghosting after move-out

**Context** — 2–3 weeks after handover: read but no reply, calls go to voicemail.

**Reading** — (1) Genuinely busy, low probability; (2) a stall until you give up; (3) they plan deductions but don't want the argument.

**Response options**
1. Structured, still-friendly demand: "I vacated on X, that's day 21. Per TA clause X, my deposit of RM3,300 should be refunded to the attached account by X. If you propose deductions, please itemise each with photos."
2. Name the next forum quietly: "If I receive neither the refund nor an itemised written response by X, I'll file a claim under the Magistrates' small claims procedure (Form 198), attaching all records and payment receipts."
3. Change the envelope, not the message: the same letter by email, marked "formal written demand" — a demand letter is the mandatory first step before civil courts.

**Pitfalls** — Repeated "when will you refund me?" with no date and no figure is not a demand. Effective questions always carry a date and a number.

**Examples** — constructed example: Two ignored WhatsApp messages, then option 2 on day 3 of silence; refund lands within 48 hours — an escalation preview carries more cost than a complaint.

### "The new owner isn't bound by your tenancy"

**Context** — The unit was sold and the buyer asks you to move out within a month.

**Reading** — (1) Wrong on the law: a stamped tenancy agreement binds the new owner for its full term; (2) deliberately pressuring you to move early; (3) misled by the agent.

**Response options**
1. Show the paper: "My tenancy agreement is stamped and runs until X date; as new owner you continue it on the same terms. Please send any proposed change in writing."
2. Convert to negotiation: "If the new owner needs it for own stay, we can discuss early termination: compensation of about 1.5 months' rent, full deposit refunded, I move out by X."
3. Ask for paper only: "Please send your request for early move-out in writing." — Most verbal evictions dissolve after this sentence.

**Pitfalls** — Packing up on a verbal promise. Nothing leaves the unit — no keys, no deposit conversation — until a written compensation and refund offer is in hand.

**Examples** — constructed example: Tenant sends option 1 with a photo of the stamped clause; the buyer replies that evening "sorry for the misunderstanding, see you at the expiry date."

### The mid-tenancy rent increase

**Context** — Mid-month call: "Rents all went up in this area, add RM200 from next month."

**Reading** — (1) A contractual breach: without a rent-review clause, rent cannot change mid-tenancy (Evidence-backed, core entry 9); (2) a preview of the renewal negotiation; (3) a verbal probe to avoid putting it in writing.

**Response options**
1. Lock onto the term: "The TA runs to X; rent is fixed until then. Let's start renewal talks at the beginning of next month, rent and tenure together."
2. Counter with comparables at renewal: "Same area, similar units are listed RM1,600–1,700 (screenshots attached). I'll renew two years at RM1,600 — you save the vacant period and agent fee."
3. Written floor only: "Please send your renewal terms in writing; I'll respond within three days."

**Pitfalls** — Blocking "the increase now" but arriving at renewal empty-handed. Renewal is the real negotiation; no comparable listings in hand means accepting whatever is offered.

**Examples** — constructed example: Landlord calls for +RM200 mid-tenancy; tenant takes option 1. Near expiry the offer returns as "renew at RM1,600 for two years?" — same negotiation, correct venue.

### Lockout, utilities cut, "your things are on the kerb"

**Context** — You owe rent or stay past expiry and the landlord threatens to change locks or remove belongings.

**Reading** — (1) In Malaysia that is unlawful self-help: recovery of possession must go through the courts (Specific Relief Act 1950 s.7(2)); (2) almost always pressure talk, rarely carried out; (3) they are actually filing for a possession order — in which case there is even less reason for them to rush.

**Response options**
1. Cite calmly: "Under s.7(2) of the Specific Relief Act 1950 possession comes only via the courts. Changing locks or removing my belongings is self-help and exposes you to a counterclaim."
2. Evidence first: photograph the door, locks, chat, timestamp; upload to the cloud. Never block the door with your body.
3. Close the money issue (if arrears are real): "I'll pay the RM1,800 (two months) on X; in exchange you confirm in writing no utilities will be cut and the matter is closed."

**Pitfalls** — Fighting in person or retaliating in kind: the right stance is recorder, not brawler; fixed evidence, then paper and court.

**Examples** — constructed example: "Pay tomorrow or I change the locks." Tenant replies with option 1; that night: "OK, I'll wait for your transfer."

### Renewal-offer urgency theatre

**Context** — Group message: "The unit is in demand, viewings next week, lock your renewal in the next two days."

**Reading** — (1) Real viewings, short window; (2) manufactured scarcity to compress your counter-offer; (3) they already have other plans (own stay, sale) and are softening the ground.

**Response options**
1. Verify, then counter: "I checked three comparable listings nearby (RM1,550–1,750, screenshots). I'll sign two years at RM1,600 today — cheaper for you than six weeks vacant."
2. Price your own record: "Two years, on-time every month, zero complaints. Same rent for two more years and you skip vacancy, cleaning and agent fees."
3. Walk cleanly: "Thanks, I won't renew. Notice served per the TA; I'll vacate on X."

**Pitfalls** — Accepting the first number because "people are viewing". Vacancy cost is real, but scarcity speech and P&L scarcity are different things.

**Examples** — constructed example: "Three groups have booked viewings." Tenant sends option 1; reply that night: "OK, RM1,600, two years."

### Landlord lane: chasing rent with three sentences

**Context** — You're the landlord, rent is 2 days late, "transferring soon".

**Reading** — (1) Bank delay or oversight; (2) a cash squeeze that will settle; (3) habitual delay — months two and three are the real risk.

**Response options**
1. Polite deadline: "The X rental was due on X and hasn't arrived. Please transfer by X; late payment per the TA is RM50 per month."
2. Written escalation preview: "Now X days overdue. If not settled by X, I'll file under the Magistrates' small claims procedure (Form 198 — no lawyer needed)."
3. Itemised deposit offset: "This month's shortfall has been deducted from the deposit (RM1,500); please top the deposit back up by X of next month." Compliant deduction, permanent record.

**Pitfalls** — Cutting utilities to squeeze payment: that crosses the unlawful self-help line and the counterclaim will exceed the arrears. Threats are never the cheaper option.

**Examples** — constructed example: Two days late; landlord sends option 1. Rent and the RM50 late fee both arrive that day — a visible rule beats an angry call.

### Landlord lane: recovering possession lawfully

**Context** — You need the tenant out (own stay, sale, breach) and want a no-footgun sequence.

**Reading** — (1) In-term breach: written breach notice per TA clause; (2) expiry approaching: Notice to Quit for the TA period (commonly 1–2 months); (3) refusal to move: only a court possession order — executed by court officers, never by you.

**Response options**
1. Template the notice: state the clause relied on, the breach facts, and the remedy period; serve by registered mail + door posting + WhatsApp, photographing each.
2. Log every contact: service mode, dates, every reply; if they don't move, a second notice marked "final deadline X".
3. Announce, don't threaten: "If you haven't vacated by X, I will apply to court for a possession order. Everything will be by the book — no utility cuts, no removal of belongings."

**Pitfalls** — Treating a hallway "you'll move out this month, right?" as notice. Courts require written service and deadlines; carrying out a tenant's suitcase tonight is the single most expensive step in the whole chain.

**Examples** — constructed example: Landlord serves a 30-day Notice to Quit by registered mail, door posting and WhatsApp. On day X+4 after expiry, the day the court receipt arrives, the tenant calls the agent to renegotiate. Procedure's weight replaces emotion.

### Both lanes: convert every verbal yes into text

**Context** — Any phone call, any corridor chat where something was agreed.

**Reading** — Verbal agreements are unprovable; the point isn't trust, it's having a quotable line three months later.

**Response options**
1. Post-call summary: "Confirming our call: you'll arrange a handyman, Saturday morning. Reply if anything differs."
2. Lock it in the moment: "Could you just send that as a WhatsApp note? I'll confirm back."
3. Monthly light log: "Meter 2,381 / 56,402; no repairs pending this month." Cheap, continuous evidence.

**Pitfalls** — Curated screenshots only. In the small claims court a continuous record beats a highlight reel; export the full chat, don't trim.

**Examples** — constructed example: Landlord verbally agrees "I'll cover the painting". Tenant follows up with a summary text; landlord replies "correct". Two months later the RM600 invoice is reimbursed in full — those two messages are the whole basis.
