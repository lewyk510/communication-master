### "Why didn't you come home for dinner?"

**Context** — a parent's WeChat message or phone call after weeks of missed family dinners. Surface question, actual loneliness.

**Reading** — (1) missing you plainly; (2) building a filial-duty account they will cash later; (3) comparing you to a cousins' family they just heard about.

**Response options**
1. Commit to a date, not a vibe: "Team sign-off is the 15th; I'll be home dinner that Friday."
2. Convert concern into shared topic: "Send me your claypot recipe, I'll try it this weekend."
3. Fix a recurring call slot and say the weekday out loud.

**Pitfalls** — "I'm busy" reads as an excuse no matter how true; over-promising then failing deepens the ledger. `Practice heuristic`

**Examples** — Mum: "When are you coming for dinner?" → "The 15th, and prepare the mushrooms I like." `constructed example`

### "Auntie's son just bought a BMW..."

**Context** — reunion table or family group chat, comparison-by-cousin season.

**Reading** — mostly a face transfer: your parents need a reportable achievement in front of the relatives; rarely a real demand that you buy a car.

**Response options**
1. Acknowledge the value, redirect the metric: "Savings are going well; I got promoted too."
2. Praise the cousin, then resettle: "His family helped with the down payment; I'm doing mine solo."
3. Long-term: arm your parents with a one-liner about what you do ("she's in AI").

**Pitfalls** — debating loudly "a car is a depreciating asset" takes face from everyone at the table, including your own parents. `constructed example`

### Gift taboos: clocks, umbrellas, pears, the number four

**Context** — choosing gifts for elders, in-laws, or the sick; CNY hampers, hospital visits.

**Reading** — homophone taboos are live social rules, not jokes: clock = 送终 (attending a death), umbrella = 散 (separation), pear = 离 (parting), four ≈ 死. `Evidence-backed`

**Response options**
1. Safe zone: red packaging, even quantities, festive food, quality tea.
2. Accidentally bought pears? Eat them together on the spot, cut open, and the homophone dissolves. `Practice heuristic`
3. For weddings and newborn visits, cash in even numbers is standard.

**Pitfalls** — a white-flower or clock gift at a hospital visit is thanked aloud and remembered for a decade. `constructed example`

### "You're still young, listen to us"

**Context** — career, relationship, or moving-out discussions blocked by an age argument.

**Reading** — (1) reasserting decision-rights over that territory; (2) real fear: you will suffer and nobody can catch you.

**Response options**
1. Catch the fear first: "I know you're worried I'll be cheated on rent; I checked the landlord's record."
2. Shrink the dispute to one concrete item: "let's just set a savings floor together."
3. Offer a trial clause: "I'll do it your way for six weeks, then we review with real numbers."

**Pitfalls** — citing other families' rebellions as evidence only threatens them. `Practice heuristic`

**Examples** — Dad: "A girl shouldn't live alone." → "That's exactly my fear, so the condo has guards; visit once before you judge." `constructed example`

### 催婚 / "When are you getting married?"

**Context** — CNY, weddings, family chat groups; the peak-pressure seasons.

**Reading** — the word "交代" (account to give others) reveals the engine: your single status is their open face-debt to other relatives.

**Response options**
1. Humour deflection: "Currently interviewing; help me shortlist."
2. Reframe success on their own value fields: health, salary, savings.
3. One calm boundary sentence, repeated identically every year: "This one I decide; you'll be the first to know."

**Pitfalls** — arguing marriage-equality theory with an aunt at the reunion dinner is a public face-loss for her and escalates pressure onto your parents. `Practice heuristic`

**Examples** — 三姑 asks again → "Still interviewing! If you meet a good engineer girl, let me know." `constructed example`

### "Big sister, mummy suffers, don't be petty"

**Context** — long-running unequal sibling duties smoothed over with harmony talk.

**Reading** — sometimes genuine fairness-blindness, sometimes the parent buying back guilt.

**Response options**
1. Concede once, name the pattern once: "I'll do it this time; going forward, duty rosters get written."
2. Substitute facts for feelings: "I was the one driving mum to dialysis for two years; I count, not for revenge, for finance."
3. Bring in a neutral elder (uncle) for the money round.

**Pitfalls** — dressing up as a doormat until the resentment surfaces at a funeral. `constructed example`

### The eldest's open wallet

**Context** — the eldest child is expected to fund a younger sibling's education or startup.

**Reading** — refusal reads as betrayal of the kinship ledger even when the venture is obviously bad.

**Response options**
1. Substitute a form, "I can't give cash; I can give skills/contacts": tutoring, co-signing a cheap loan is NOT recommended. `Practice heuristic`
2. Convert a token into an explicit gift: "This 2k is a gift, not a loan; no returning it."
3. Pre-set an annual cap so future asks are docketed to a rule, not to your kindness.

**Pitfalls** — publicising your subsidy in the family chat invites future precedent claims from other siblings. `Practice heuristic`

### Funeral condolence: the details people actually check

**Context** — attending a relative's or friend's parent's funeral in Malaysia.

**Reading** — your envelope and outfit are read as a public statement of respect.

**Response options**
1. Chinese funerals: 帛金 in a white envelope (customarily 100+1-type odd amounts), sign the condolence book, muted colours only. `Evidence-backed`, `Practice heuristic`
2. Malay funerals: go early, offer salam condolences, join or stand aside respectfully at the prayers.
3. Indian/cremation services: white clothing, modest and quiet, no pointing; garlands per the family's priests' guidance. `anecdotal`

**Pitfalls** — red is celebration red anywhere in this region: wearing red at a wake is the classic disaster. `Evidence-backed`

**Examples** — unsure of the family's denomination → ask one line: "Any dress or family-only customs I should watch?" `constructed example`

### Multiethnic wedding guest moves (Malaysia)

**Context** — invited to a Malay bersanding, a Chinese banquet, or an Indian temple wedding the same month.

**Reading** — each protocol has different non-negotiables; one etiquette memory misfires across all three.

**Response options**
1. Malay: modest dress, check invitation for reception timing, arrive before bersanding; green/blue sizeable-envelopes community practice. `Evidence-backed`, `anecdotal`
2. Chinese: tea ceremony seniority order; even-numbered cash gifts.
3. Indian: shoes off inside the temple, cash figures like 501. `anecdotal`

**Pitfalls** — alcohol gifts: fine to bring to some Chinese homes, absolutely not to Muslim households.

### Ageing parents: who does what

**Context** — after a parent's hospitalisation, sibling duty negotiation.

**Reading** — breakdown is usually invisibility of contributions, not the money itself.

**Response options**
1. Open a shared ledger in the family chat: who paid what, photo of receipt.
2. Rotate a single primary contact per month instead of everybody vaguely responsible.
3. Leave decision rights with the parent; you automate the logistics.

**Pitfalls** — letting one member pay and drive simultaneously, then calling her "calculating" when she raises it. `Practice heuristic`

**Examples** — chat: "Mum's MRI: RM1,800, receipt in album." `constructed example`

### Boundaries in a face culture that survive

**Context** — recurring interference in your marriage, fertility, career, or how you raise your kids.

**Reading** — intrusion is mostly habit never blocked before, not active malice.

**Response options**
1. Pre-announce the line once, gently, then stop relitigating it each flare-up.
2. Keep warmth high everywhere else: festivals, birthdays, visits run as normal, proving the line isn't love-withdrawal.
3. Pre-set frequency and money ceilings yearly, so disputes resolve by rule, not by volume.

**Pitfalls** — boundaries announced only mid-fight look like weapons, not principles. `Practice heuristic`
