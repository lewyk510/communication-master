### Would be great if someone could…

**Context** — manager emails the team; no name. Classic English corporate directive.

**Reading** — 1) genuinely open call; 2) "someone" means *you* (Searle-type indirect directive, Evidence-backed: searle capture); 3) testing who volunteers.

**Response options**
1. Clarify assignments: "I can take the first half — shall I note it as owned?" — cites [[playbooks/p2-subtext-decoder/core]]
2. Accept conditionally: "I'll own it if the design freeze stays Friday." — cites [[concepts/c1-subtext-implicature/core]]
3. Ask the discriminator: "Is this blocking something this week?" — cites [[playbooks/p2-subtext-decoder/core]]

**Pitfalls** — staying silent because "someone" could be anyone; the silence reads as refusal.

**Examples** — `constructed example`: PM posts it in the sprint channel; the two people who don't reply get their next tasks scaled back.

### I'll get back to you on this

**Context** — any request postponed without commitment; email, calls, meetings.

**Reading** — 1) real scheduling; 2) soft no; 3) needs to consult someone first.

**Response options**
1. Anchor a date: "Great — Thursday works as a checkpoint?" — cites [[playbooks/p2-subtext-decoder/core]]
2. Offer to shrink the question: "To make it easy: yes/no on the 2-week trial only?" — cites [[concepts/c1-subtext-implicature/core]]
3. Let it lapse politely: send nothing; treat silence past 72h as answer 2. — cites [[playbooks/p2-subtext-decoder/core]]

**Pitfalls** — treating it as a promise; it is a channel-opening gesture at best (Evidence-backed: culturally-dependent-no capture lists this as a noncommittal form).

**Examples** — `constructed example`: vendor says it after a proposal; the only client who later wrote back had first sent a question, not a deadline.

### No worries if not!

**Context** — request softened with an explicit opt-out; very common in en-US work chat.

**Reading** — 1) genuinely low-stakes; 2) face-preserving pressure, the softer the sentence the higher the ask's importance (Practice heuristic); 3) apologizing for an unreasonable ask in advance.

**Response options**
1. Take it literally once: "Sure, can do." — cites [[playbooks/p2-subtext-decoder/core]]
2. Trade scope: "I can do it today only if the report moves to Monday." — cites [[concepts/c1-subtext-implicature/core]]
3. Name the pattern lightly: "You say that every time you're overloaded — what's the real deadline?" — cites [[playbooks/p2-subtext-decoder/core]]

**Pitfalls** — rejecting the softener ("no, I won't") when outcome 2 was intended; the relationship cost lands on you.

**Examples** — `constructed example`: "No worries if not, but could you look at this deck tonight?" — the deck was for a 9am client call.

### K / Thx / 👍

**Context** — one-word text replies; dating, family, colleague channels.

**Reading** — 1) agreed, moving on; 2) irritation or distance compared to their baseline ("ok honey!" → "k" is a shift); 3) busy, placeholder only. Tone loss in text makes this ambiguous (Practice heuristic, anecdotal: decode-his-text capture).

**Response options**
1. Check baseline before checking vibes: compare against their last 20 replies, not your feelings. — cites [[playbooks/p2-subtext-decoder/core]]
2. Low-cost probe: "All good? Want me to handle X so you're free?" — cites [[concepts/c1-subtext-implicature/core]]

**Pitfalls** — treating "k" as a breakup signal; the three-layers rule says your alarm story is layer 3, not layer 2 (anecdotal: capture).

**Examples** — `constructed example`: daily-texter sends "k" for two days; turn out to be a family hospital visit, not conflict.

### Per my last email…

**Context** — email thread where you already asked and got nothing.

**Reading** — surface: restart. Subtext: 1) mild reproach ("you ignored me"); 2) escalation prelude before going over heads; 3) genuine assumption that the mail got buried.

**Response options**
1. Answer the substance immediately, ignore the jab: reply with exactly what was requested. — cites [[playbooks/p2-subtext-decoder/core]]
2. De-escalate with cover: "This thread is long — pasting the one question here." — cites [[concepts/c1-subtext-implicature/core]]
3. Name it neutrally: "No reply earlier — happy to align on a call if faster." — cites [[playbooks/p2-subtext-decoder/core]]

**Pitfalls** — mirroring the passive aggression; it converts a workflow issue into a status fight.

**Examples** — `constructed example`: "Per my last email" answered within an hour with an apology: the mail truly was in their promotions folder.

### We should catch up sometime

**Context** — networking events, distant friends, exiting conversations.

**Reading** — 1) politeness closer, no intent (most common); 2) genuine wish but no priority; 3) door left open on purpose after a falling-out.

**Response options**
1. Convert or confirm: "Sometime" rarely happens — "Lunch next Tuesday?" — cites [[playbooks/p2-subtext-decoder/core]]
2. Accept as ritual: warm ack, move on, re-initiate only if patterns repeat. — cites [[concepts/c1-subtext-implicature/core]]

**Pitfalls** — waiting for a calendar invite from a ritual closer; check who initiated last time before re-sending one.

**Examples** — `constructed example`: "sometime" said to you twice this year; both times you offered a concrete date, both accepted.

### I need to think about it

**Context** — sales, proposals, personal asks (moving in, merging budgets).

**Reading** — 1) genuinely weighing trade-offs; 2) no, avoiding saying it; 3) needs a third party's sign-off (partner, boss).

**Response options**
1. Give one clean reason to return: "If price is the blocker I can split it into two payments." — cites [[playbooks/p2-subtext-decoder/core]]
2. Set a soft expiry: "It's yours until Friday either way." — cites [[concepts/c1-subtext-implicature/core]]
3. Name the missing info: "Is it cost, timing, or fit? I can send one page per option." — cites [[playbooks/p2-subtext-decoder/core]]

**Pitfalls** — pushing daily; converts reading 2 into documented refusal.

**Examples** — `constructed example`: tenant-to-be says it about a lease; a single follow-up question ("is it the deposit?") unlocks the real concern: pets.

### Fine.

**Context** — spoken or text; conflict or post-conflict conversations.

**Reading** — 1) truly fine (rare in conflict); 2) not fine, conversation paused on their terms; 3) concession to end an exhausting exchange (paralanguage decides: see [[concepts/c11-paralanguage/core]]).

**Response options**
1. In text: don't decode solo — switch channel: "Voice note or call in 10?" — cites [[playbooks/p2-subtext-decoder/core]]
2. In person: read tone first, then words; a flat "fine" with closed posture is a pause, not an agreement. — cites [[concepts/c6-nonverbal/core]]
3. Offer a delayed reopening: "Okay — want to park this until tomorrow?" — cites [[concepts/c1-subtext-implicature/core]]

**Pitfalls** — booking "fine" as agreement in conflict contexts; the costliest one-word misread in relationships.

**Examples** — `constructed example`: partner says "fine" twice on the phone; third probe reveals a broken promise from weeks earlier.
