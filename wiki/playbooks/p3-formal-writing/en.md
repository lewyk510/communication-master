### The seven-element skeleton

**Context** — every formal letter (complaint, appeal, refund, application, refusal, negotiation) shares one skeleton; auditors and tribunals check elements, not prose.

**Reading** — sender / recipient / date / subject / body / demand / enclosures, all present = processable; any missing = delay or dismissal.

**Response options**
1. Email: put sender block in your signature footer, date in Subject line.
2. Hard copy: full letterhead top-right, recipient block top-left, date beneath.
3. Portal submission: paste the body; enclosures become numbered uploads.

**Pitfalls** — forgetting the complaint reference number in the Subject line is the #1 cause of "we cannot locate your file".

**Examples** — constructed example: `Subject: Formal Complaint — Prepaid SIM RM60 unusable — Receipt #A001 — Refund demanded`.

### Reference-number threading

**Context** — invoices, order IDs, ticket IDs, previous complaint refs must appear in both Subject and body.

**Reading** — regulator case officers match facts to documents by these strings first.

**Response options**
1. Thread format: `(Ref: #A001; prior tickets #T-772, #T-810)`.
2. Log every contact: date, channel, agent name, ref given.
3. Registered/receipted email gives you an independently timed "Ref".

**Pitfalls** — starting a fresh thread per follow-up breaks the audit chain; always reply-all on the same thread.

**Examples** — constructed example: "Following my complaints lodged 2026-09-12 (ticket #T-772) and 2026-09-24 (#T-810), neither answered in writing …".

### The firm-but-polite register (FTC-aligned)

**Context** — FTC's official sample letter instructs complainants to "be polite and reasonable" and "tell the business what you want" (Evidence-backed, raw/p3-formal-writing/f-ftc-complaint-letter.md).

**Reading** — firmness lives in facts, deadlines and the escalation notice; politeness lives in person, tense and closing.

**Response options**
1. Neutral verbs: "the service I paid for was not delivered" — never "you scammers".
2. One primary demand; repairs/apologies at most secondary.
3. Keep "I remain open to an amicable resolution within that period" as the closing line.

**Pitfalls** — sarcasm and ALL-CAPS give the recipient's legal team an easy dismissal; threats to persons are void, threats to escalate are leverage.

**Examples** — constructed example: "I am constrained to note that four written enquiries between 12 and 28 September have gone unanswered."

### Complaint letter — full skeleton

**Context** — first letter to the provider (ladder rung 1).

**Reading** — timeline + right breached + demand + escalation notice.

**Response options**
1. Standard body; see core.md §8.1 for the canonical full skeleton.
2. Merchant ignored you: retitle "Escalation — Second Notice".
3. Foreign merchant: pivot demand to card chargeback (core.md §8.3).

**Pitfalls** — asking for "compensation" without a figure invites the cheapest remedy; never miss the 14-day style deadline.

**Examples** — constructed example body: "On 2026-09-01 I purchased a prepaid SIM (Annex A: receipt #A001). Validity was never disclosed at sale or top-up. On 2026-09-10 a RM60 top-up failed to deliver service (Annex B). I hereby request a full refund of RM60.00 within fourteen (14) days, failing which I will lodge a complaint with the relevant Malaysian redress channel."

### Appeal letter — full skeleton

**Context** — overturning or reopening a negative decision (school, employer, government body).

**Reading** — grounds must attack the decision, not restate the grievance; each ground ties to evidence or to a rule/procedural defect.

**Response options**
1. Three-part grounds: (a) merit, (b) new evidence, (c) procedural defect.
2. Request a written decision with a deadline (14 days is standard practice heuristic).
3. Cite the specific guideline section that permits appeal — it converts your plea into a process.

**Pitfalls** — appeals re-argued with only emotion fail; a deadline-free appeal dies in a queue.

**Examples** — constructed example: "I respectfully appeal against the decision of 2026-09-20 (Ref: #D-114) on the following grounds: (a) …; (b) new evidence enclosed as Annex D; (c) the decision issued without the 7-day notice required by §X."

### Application letter — full skeleton

**Context** — jobs, schools, government filings; mirror each stated requirement to one proof.

**Reading** — requirement→evidence mapping is what screeners actually scan for.

**Response options**
1. Position letters: vacancy code in Subject, availability + commencement date in body.
2. Government filings: permit/licence number, forms and fee receipts annexed.
3. Add a one-line call-to-action: "I would welcome an interview at your convenience."

**Pitfalls** — listing duties instead of matching requirements; forgetting enclosures list kills the file.

**Examples** — constructed example: "The advertised role requires (1) CMDB experience — four years at {{employer}}; (2) ITIL v4 — certified 2025 (Annex B)."

### Formal refusal — full skeleton

**Context** — declining a request while preserving the relationship and avoiding waiver or admission.

**Reading** — the four moves: acknowledge, decline, reason tied to a rule, offer an alternative or correct channel.

**Response options**
1. Offer what you CAN do inside the same letter — a refusal plus an alternative reads as service, not defeat.
2. Reserve rights: "This letter does not constitute an admission or waiver of any rights."
3. State any reconsideration condition ("should the above documentation be provided, the request may be revisited").

**Pitfalls** — over-explaining creates appealable inconsistency; apologies that admit fault ("we clearly erred") become evidence.

**Examples** — constructed example: "After careful consideration, we are unable to refund transactions older than 90 days, per our published refund policy §4 (Annex A). We can however offer a 90-day service credit; the correct channel to reopen this is the [regulator's] portal."

### Negotiation email — full skeleton

**Context** — settlement-style drafting (debt, severance, commercial dispute).

**Reading** — mutual goal → leverage (brief, evidenced) → package proposal → concession boundaries + expiry.

**Response options**
1. Package format: "we do X (~x%), you do Y (~y%), in full and final settlement".
2. Add lapse clause: "this proposal lapses on {{date}}" — real deadlines extract real replies.
3. Confirm authority: state you sign off internally by a date.

**Pitfalls** — single-item asks invite single-item counters; revealing your floor first weakens the package.

**Examples** — constructed example: "Proposal: we release the retainer claim (RM3,000) on payment of the outstanding RM8,200 by 30 October, in full and final settlement. This proposal lapses on 30 October."

### Malaysia escalation ladder (regulator rungs)

**Context** — the third rung differs by sector; pick the regulator before drafting the first letter so its 14-day-style rule can be cited as leverage.

**Reading** — Evidence-backed channels (trace: raw/p3-formal-writing/): **TTPM** (Tribunal for Consumer Claims, KPDN) — consumer claims, Borang 1 in 4 copies, RM5 filing fee, online at ttpm.kpdn.gov.my, hotline 1-800-88-9811; respondent must file Borang 2 within 14 days (f-ttpm-prosedur.md). **MCMC/CFM** (comms & multimedia) — aduan.mcmc.gov.my, 1800-188-030, aduan@cfm.my (f-cfm-faq.md). **BNMLINK, Bank Negara Malaysia** (banking/insurance/takaful/e-money) — Complaints Unit of the FSP first, then 14 days without a final decision → BNMLINK: bnmlink.bnm.gov.my, 1-300-88-5465 (f-bnm-complaint.md). **SSM** — e-Complaint (ssm.com.my), +603-7721 4000, enquiry@ssm.com.my. **Consumer bodies** — FOMCA +603-78764648 / fomca@fomca.org.my, NCCC (nccc.org.my), Consumers Association of Penang consumer.org.my 04-8299511 (cca.json).

**Response options**
1. Consumer purchase dispute → cite TTPM in the rung-1 letter's escalation notice.
2. Telco/broadband → cite MCMC/CFM portal and hotline.
3. Bank refusal → demand the Complaints Unit's final decision in writing, then refer to BNMLINK.

**Pitfalls** — BNM explicitly rejects cases not first taken to the provider's Complaints Unit; SSM handles the business entity, not the merchant-consumer sale.

**Examples** — constructed example: telco refund letter ends "Failing which I will lodge a complaint via the MCMC Consumer Redress Portal (aduan.mcmc.gov.my, 1800-188-030) and, if necessary, file a claim at the Tribunal for Consumer Claims."

### Internal email: "Per my last email" + cc'ing the boss

**Context** — half-formal internal threads; the other side replies "Per my last email", or moves your boss to cc.

**Reading** — "Per my last email" is a public ledger entry ("you didn't read / you missed it") — pressure, not a question; a cc to your boss raises the stakes and builds a record. Read the cc list to see which tier you've been placed on (tier-2: raw/p3-formal-writing/f-cnbc-passive-email.md, f-wtj-passive-email.md).

**Response options**
1. Re-read first, reply to facts only: "Updated per point 2 of your {{date}} email — see below; flag anything I missed."
2. Move the friction off-thread: "Can we take 5 minutes on a call to align? I'm free {{slot}}."
3. When a record is needed, reply in memo form: one ask + one deadline.

**Pitfalls** — firing back the same day, especially with the other side's boss on cc; treating "per my last email" as a personal attack instead of a re-read prompt.

**Examples** — constructed example: they cc your manager and write "Per my last email, please confirm"; you reply with data only, then move to a call.
